*Professor of Philosophical Anthropology, Luna University (2095–2156)*

**I. Biography**

Born in Munich, Guth studied philosophy at LMU München (2113–2118) before emigrating to Luna in 2121, where he completed graduate work in anthropology (2121–2125). He specialized in ethics within closed systems and interstellar moral philosophy.

He became known for moderating twenty-three major interdisciplinary debates without ever revealing a personal preference for either side — a discipline he attributed less to neutrality than to a conviction that "the truth is not in the middle. It is in the listening."

**II. Personal**

Guth was unmarried but partnered for over two decades with Dr. Takeshi Yamamoto, a linguist, with whom he lived in Luna City. He suffered from chronic pulmonary edema, common among Earth-to-Luna migrants, and discreetly carried a supplemental oxygen unit. He collected physical philosophy volumes — rare off-Earth.

**III. The 2147 Debate**

Guth moderated the legendary 2147 dialectic between Dr. Elif Demir and Nemeth Tiefenborn (OTA-CUL-0013-2147-EN), the only figure both parties accepted as moderator. His 2142 work, *"Ethics of Enclosed Existence,"* had already demonstrated an unusual ability to represent both positions fairly.

**IV. Death**

Guth died on 15 June 2156 in the Luna Decompression Event, Sector 7 of Luna University Campus, alongside 846 others. His final message to Yamamoto, sent two hours before his death, references "the third model" and "the Foundation that has no form" — see OTA-RED-0015-2156-EN, Source 4.

**V. Posthumous Work**

Yamamoto published Guth's incomplete notes in 2160 as *"The Third Position: Notes Toward a Synthesis"* (see OTA-RED-0016-2160-EN). The final twelve pages of the original manuscript were never found.

**[KN-5.1]**

Luna University named the "Guth Hall of Discourse" in his honor in 2157. Yamamoto founded the Guth Foundation for Peaceful Dialogue the same year, and in 2157 installed three empty chairs in the Resonance Cathedral on Ceres — an installation whose meaning has never been explained. Those who sit in the third chair report feeling "strange." No one can describe how.

— T.P.K.
