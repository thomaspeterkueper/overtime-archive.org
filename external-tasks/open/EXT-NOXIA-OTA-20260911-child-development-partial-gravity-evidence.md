## Anlass

The existing reproductive evidence chain in `OTA-SCI-0086-2026-DE` covers pregnancy, childbirth and early development, but NOXIA and Generation Mars also require a scientifically bounded model of what happens when children grow from infancy through puberty under microgravity, lunar gravity (~0.16 g) or Martian gravity (~0.38 g).

As of 2026, this must not be converted into an assumed safe-gravity threshold. Current literature explicitly reports that mammalian reproduction and development under sustained lunar/Martian partial gravity have not been directly evaluated and that the relevant gravitational thresholds remain unknown.

## Research question

Develop an evidence dossier covering development from the neonatal period through adolescence in:

1. near-microgravity / orbital habitats;
2. lunar gravity (~0.16 g);
3. Martian gravity (~0.38 g);
4. intermittent artificial gravity;
5. continuous artificial gravity.

Separate clearly:

- established human physiology;
- animal evidence;
- cellular / mechanobiological evidence;
- analogue and simulation evidence;
- inference;
- unresolved unknowns.

## Required evidence domains

### A. Skeletal growth

Assess bone mineralization, growth plates, long-bone development, spinal loading, craniofacial development, fracture/injury risk and whether childhood mechanical loading creates irreversible differences in adult skeletal capacity.

Do not infer that low gravity simply produces taller humans unless evidence supports it.

### B. Muscle and motor development

Assess muscle mass, muscle-fiber development, tendon/ligament loading, strength, coordination, gait acquisition, posture and locomotor skill development under reduced loading.

### C. Vestibular and sensorimotor development

Assess otolith/vestibular development, gravistatic responses, balance, eye-head coordination, spatial orientation, motion adaptation and the consequences of changing gravity environments during critical developmental windows.

### D. Cardiovascular development

Assess blood-volume regulation, cardiac growth/loading, vascular development, baroreflex and orthostatic conditioning, including the implications for later transfer to higher gravity.

### E. Neurodevelopment and cognition

Assess evidence for altered neural development, cortical/synaptic morphology, sensorimotor integration, sleep/circadian effects and any supported cognitive or behavioural consequences. Avoid extrapolating animal findings directly to deterministic human outcomes.

### F. Endocrine development and puberty

Assess growth hormone/IGF axis, puberty timing, sex-hormone systems, reproductive maturation and whether altered gravity/radiation could affect later fertility. Explicitly identify where evidence is absent.

### G. Immune, microbiome and metabolic development

Assess immune maturation, microbiome development, metabolism, nutrition requirements and interactions with closed-habitat environments.

### H. Radiation during childhood

Treat radiation as a separate environmental variable from gravity. Summarize developmental sensitivity, cumulative exposure concerns and evidence gaps without inventing new dose limits.

### I. Gravity transition / migration

Assess the expected consequences of moving a child or adolescent between 0 g, 0.16 g, 0.38 g and 1 g. Distinguish acute adaptation from possible developmental constraints. Include the question whether a Mars- or Moon-raised person could safely tolerate Earth gravity after years of development elsewhere; do not presume the answer.

### J. Developmental windows

Identify which periods may be especially sensitive: infancy, acquisition of standing/walking, early childhood, prepubertal growth spurt and puberty/adolescence.

## Required deliverables

1. evidence-confidence matrix by organ system and gravity regime;
2. age-stage matrix from birth to adulthood;
3. distinction between reversible adaptation and potentially persistent developmental change;
4. known/unknown matrix for 0 g, Moon 0.16 g, Mars 0.38 g and artificial gravity;
5. research priorities needed before any safe-gravity threshold can be claimed;
6. implications that KUEPER Engineering may use as design constraints, explicitly labelled provisional where evidence is absent;
7. candidate additions or extension proposal for `OTA-SCI-0086-2026-DE`, or a new SCI document if scope requires separation.

## Evidence boundary

No claim should be made that childhood development is validated as safe at 0 g, 0.16 g or 0.38 g. Artificial gravity should be treated as a plausible engineering countermeasure, not as a medically validated prescription. The lack of controlled long-duration mammalian partial-gravity developmental studies must remain visible in the final document.
