## Request

Review the Engineering closure for canonical adoption.

### Proposed Ceres architecture

Use a staged artificial-gravity capability:

`C2 intermittent AG -> C5 pregnancy + early-childhood + training AG -> C3 continuous family/medical rotating zone`.

Canonicalize this only as an engineering robustness path.

Do **not** canonicalize:

- a safe gravity threshold;
- a required daily g-dose;
- a pediatric rpm limit;
- a claim that ~0.03 g is sufficient;
- a claim that 1 g is necessary.

Illustrative mechanical design points are available in the Engineering source and must remain tagged as rotation physics, not biological prescription.

### Proposed freighter architecture

Introduce mission-certification classes:

- `FB0`: uncrewed / no reproductive-medical capability;
- `FB1`: crewed long-haul emergency-birth capability;
- `FB2`: planned-pregnancy/passenger capability;
- `FB3`: family-certified long-duration transport.

Current `ENG-SCV-0001` remains FB0 unless a crewed variant is separately defined.

### Canonical technical implications

Ceres/family infrastructure should reserve:

- scalable AG structure and utilities;
- protected maternal/neonatal medical volume;
- pediatric monitoring;
- gravity-transition support;
- ordinary child movement/loading volume;
- destination-gravity pre-adaptation;
- rotation-loss degraded mode.

FB1+ freighters require:

- prenatal diagnostics;
- restrained obstetric work zone;
- fluid/blood capture;
- neonatal resuscitation;
- defined operative/anaesthesia capability;
- transfusion strategy;
- radiation shelter access;
- ECLSS margin for post-birth occupancy;
- age-appropriate maneuver restraints;
- precomputed diversion logic.

### Open evidence

Retain as open:

- minimum safe gravity;
- exposure duration/duty cycle;
- prenatal vs postnatal sensitivity;
- pediatric rotation tolerance;
- developmental reversibility;
- interaction with radiation;
- transition preparation duration.
